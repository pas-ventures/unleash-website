The bench

What being on the bench means

Two things, and you can do either one or both.

Mentor

Give an hour to a founder in the area you actually know something about. Most of the value here is one good conversation at the right moment.

Co-invest

When a company you have been close to raises, you see it early and decide for yourself. Your own capital, your own call, deal by deal.

Matched to what you know

Founders come to you by sector and stage, so the first conversation is useful to both of you.

As much or as little as you want

Some people mentor and never invest. Some invest and mentor rarely. Both work.

The thesis

What it actually takes to return money in South Africa

For a company here to create a return for its founders and its investors, it has to work in South Africa first, be profitable in South Africa first, and be ready to expand from South Africa. That order matters, and most capital gets it the wrong way round.

It also has to be patient money. It cannot be running on someone else’s clock — the LPs of a global fund with no feel for the South African reality will always want the exit before the business is ready to give one.

And it has to come from people with skin in the game, who have built and exited businesses themselves. That is what this market asks of you, and it is why we invest our own capital.

Who this suits

People who have built something meaningful themselves

Operators who can speak from experience, and who thrive on real successes rather than on hype. If you would rather back a founder directly than write a cheque into a fund, and you are willing to give an hour to the person coming up behind you, this is the room.
